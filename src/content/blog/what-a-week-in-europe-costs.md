Switzerland's price level for restaurants and hotels is more than three times Bulgaria's. That gap is real, and it is measured. But the country that is cheapest for a hotel room is not always the country that is cheapest for a bag of groceries, and that difference can decide what your trip actually costs.

Every price level in this post comes from one source: Eurostat's price level indices for 2024, the same figures behind our free [cost per country comparison](/tools/cost-per-country/).

## How to read a price level

Eurostat compares what the same basket of goods and services costs in each country. The EU average is set at 100. A country at 120 is about 20 percent dearer than the EU average for that basket. A country at 80 is about 20 percent cheaper.

Eurostat publishes a separate index for each kind of spending. Two matter most on a trip:

- **restaurants and hotels**, which covers where you sleep and eating out
- **food and non-alcoholic beverages**, which is the food you buy in shops

## The cheap country for hotels is not the cheap country for groceries

That table uses one index. It is a fair guide if you stay in hotels and eat out. It is a weaker guide if you rent an apartment and cook.

Here are the ten countries where the two indices differ most.

| Country | Restaurants and hotels | Food from shops |
|---|---|---|
| Bulgaria | 53.2 | 88.8 |
| Denmark | 147.6 | 120.2 |
| Portugal | 75.5 | 101.5 |
| Netherlands | 124.6 | 98.9 |
| Malta | 88.7 | 112.2 |
| Iceland | 167.3 | 143.9 |
| Hungary | 71.8 | 94.8 |
| Greece | 86.4 | 105.8 |
| Belgium | 124.2 | 105.3 |
| Finland | 126.8 | 109.8 |

**Portugal** is 75.5 for restaurants and hotels, well below the EU average. For food from shops it is 101.5, slightly above it. Hotels and meals out in Portugal are cheap. Food from the shops costs about the EU average.

**Bulgaria** is the cheapest country in the dataset for restaurants and hotels, at 53.2. For food from shops it is 88.8. That is still below average, but the saving is far smaller than the hotel figure suggests.

**The Netherlands** runs the other way. It is 124.6 for restaurants and hotels, number 23 of 28 from the cheapest, and 98.9 for food from shops, just below the EU average. Denmark is similar, at 147.6 against 120.2.

## What this means for your plan

- If you will mostly stay in hotels and eat out, compare the restaurants and hotels index.
- If you will rent an apartment and cook, look at food from shops as well. The gap between the two can change which country is cheaper for you.
- A price level is a national average. Capital cities and tourist districts run above their national average, sometimes well above, and high season and low season can differ more than two countries do.
