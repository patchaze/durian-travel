**The short version.** Whether you need a Schengen visa for a short trip depends on your nationality. The EU keeps two lists in one regulation: nationalities that need a visa, and nationalities that do not. Citizens of the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland are on neither list, because free movement applies to them.

This post covers short stays only: tourism and similar trips of up to 90 days in any 180-day period in the Schengen area. It does not cover work, study, residence or any long stay.

## Where the rule comes from

The lists are in Regulation (EU) 2018/1806. Annex I lists the countries whose nationals must hold a visa when crossing the external borders. Annex II lists the countries whose nationals are exempt for stays of no more than 90 days in any 180-day period.

Durian's copy of those lists has 204 entries, and each one falls into one of four groups.

| Group | Entries | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Free movement | 31 | No visa and no ETIAS. Free movement rights apply with no time limit under the Schengen short stay rules. |
| Visa exempt | 43 | No short stay visa needed for up to 90 days in any 180 day period. |
| Visa exempt with a condition | 26 | No short stay visa needed for up to 90 days in any 180 day period, but only if a condition is met. |
| Visa required | 104 | A Schengen short stay visa is required. |

## 1. Free movement

These 31 nationalities need no visa and no ETIAS. They appear in neither annex of the regulation, because their position comes from free movement rules instead.

Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland.

## 2. Visa exempt

These 43 nationalities need no visa for a short stay of up to 90 days in any 180-day period.

Andorra, Antigua and Barbuda, Argentina, Australia, Bahamas, Barbados, Brazil, British national (Overseas), British overseas citizen (BOC), British overseas territories citizen (BOTC), British protected person (BPP), British subject (BS), Brunei, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Holy See, Honduras, Israel, Japan, Malaysia, Mauritius, Mexico, Monaco, New Zealand, Nicaragua, Panama, Paraguay, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Samoa, San Marino, Seychelles, Singapore, Solomon Islands, South Korea, Trinidad and Tobago, United Kingdom, United States, Uruguay and Venezuela.

ETIAS, a separate travel authorisation for visa-exempt travelers, is a different question. Our [ETIAS guide](/blog/what-is-etias/) covers it.

### The British categories

The United Kingdom is visa exempt. The regulation lists British nationals who are not British citizens separately, in part 3 of Annex II, and each of them is visa exempt too: British national (Overseas), British overseas citizen (BOC), British overseas territories citizen (BOTC), British protected person (BPP) and British subject (BS). If your nationality is one of these, that is the entry that applies to you.

## 3. Visa exempt, but only if a condition is met

These 26 entries are the ones that catch people out. The exemption applies only when the condition is met.

**Only with a biometric passport.** Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro and North Macedonia: only if your passport is biometric. Georgia, Kosovo, Moldova, Serbia and Ukraine: only if your passport is a biometric passport issued by that country to ICAO standards. For Serbia, passports issued by the Serbian Coordination Directorate are included. For Kosovo, a second condition in the regulation set the start date as the day ETIAS starts operations or 1 January 2024, whichever came first. That date has passed.

**Only from the date an agreement with the EU took effect.** Dominica, Grenada, Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Nauru, Palau, Peru, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Timor-Leste, Tonga, Tuvalu and the United Arab Emirates are visa free only from the date their visa exemption agreement with the European Union entered into force. Check the current position with the consulate of your main destination.

**Only with a particular passport.** Hong Kong SAR: only holders of a Hong Kong Special Administrative Region passport. Macao SAR: only holders of a Região Administrativa Especial de Macau passport. Taiwan: only holders of a Taiwan passport that includes an identity card number.

## 4. Visa required

These 104 nationalities need a Schengen short stay visa.

Afghanistan, Algeria, Angola, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Bahrain, Bangladesh, Belarus, Belize, Benin, Bhutan, Bolivia, Botswana, Burkina Faso, Burundi, Cambodia, Cameroon, Cape Verde, Central African Republic, Chad, China, Comoros, Congo, Cuba, Côte d'Ivoire, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Djibouti, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, Egypt, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Eswatini, Ethiopia, Fiji, Gabon, Ghana, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Guyana, Haiti, India, Indonesia, Iran, Iraq, Jamaica, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Kenya, Kuwait, Kyrgyzstan, Laos, Lebanon, Lesotho, Liberia, Libya, Madagascar, Malawi, Maldives, Mali, Mauritania, Mongolia, Morocco, Mozambique, Myanmar/Burma, Namibia, Nepal, Niger, Nigeria, North Korea, Oman, Pakistan, Papua New Guinea, Philippines, Qatar, Russia, Rwanda, Saudi Arabia, Senegal, Sierra Leone, Somalia, South Africa, South Sudan, Sri Lanka, Sudan, Suriname, Syria, São Tomé and Príncipe, Tajikistan, Tanzania, Thailand, The Gambia, The Palestinian Authority, Togo, Tunisia, Turkey, Turkmenistan, Uganda, Uzbekistan, Vanuatu, Vietnam, Yemen, Zambia and Zimbabwe.
